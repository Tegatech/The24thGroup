This guide defines the parent brand system for The 24th Group. It establishes the visual and verbal rules that hold the portfolio together while leaving each venture enough room to remain distinctive.

Master brandDigital-firstLiving system

One operating home. Distinct ventures. A shared standard of clarity.

The Group should feel disciplined, editorial and quietly confident. It is not a conventional corporate brand and it should not look like a generic holding company, consultancy or venture fund.

01 · Brand idea

What the Group stands for

The visual system begins with the same strategic pattern behind the portfolio: important work that is fragmented, manual or unreliable can be made clearer, more structured and more dependable.

Principle 01

Clarity

Make the important thing easy to see. Reduce noise. Use plain language, strong hierarchy and deliberate spacing.

Principle 02

Discipline

Systems should feel intentional and maintained. Consistency matters more than decorative novelty.

Principle 03

Long-term

Build identities and systems that can mature. Avoid visual decisions that depend on trends or launch hype.

02 · Logo system

The Group mark and lockup

The full lockup is the primary signature. The standalone mark is reserved for compact digital spaces, favicons, app icons and quiet ownership marks.

Primary use on white, paper and other light surfaces.
Use the supplied white lockup on dark surfaces. Do not manually recolour the black asset.
Standalone mark

Use where the full name is already clear or where space is genuinely constrained.

Clearspace rule

Give it room.

Keep surrounding type and graphic elements comfortably separated from the lockup. As a working rule, preserve clearspace at least equal to the visual height of the Group mark.

05 · Layout & composition

Strong alignment. Generous space.

The Group should feel designed through proportion and hierarchy rather than decoration. Pages should have a clear reading order and enough breathing room for information to feel deliberate.

01

Use a visible grid

Align headings, labels, body text and media to shared columns. Avoid floating elements that feel placed by eye without a system.

02

Let whitespace carry hierarchy

Separate sections with space before introducing boxes. Cards should be used when they clarify grouping, not as the default container for every piece of content.

03

Keep surfaces restrained

Prefer white, warm paper, thin rules and occasional black fields. Avoid gradients, glassmorphism, heavy shadows and over-rounded UI.

04

Make data feel editorial

Numbers, status and evidence should be easy to scan without turning Group communication into a dashboard aesthetic.

06 · Imagery & graphics

Meaning before decoration.

The Group itself does not need a fixed photography style as expressive as its ventures. Visual material should explain work, people, systems or context rather than fill space.

Photography

Documentary over stock

Prefer real environments, real work and real people. Avoid generic corporate teamwork imagery and obvious stock metaphors.

Graphic language

Simple geometry

Thin circles, lines, dots, grids and technical annotations can support composition. Use them sparsely and structurally.

Product visuals

Show the system

When a venture is the subject, use its own product and brand language. The Group should frame the work rather than visually overwrite it.

07 · Voice

Direct, precise and calm.

The Group voice should sound like an operator who understands the work. It should be ambitious without hype, technical without jargon for its own sake, and confident without grandstanding.

Prefer

“We build where important work is still fragmented, manual or unreliable.”

Avoid

“We are revolutionising industries through world-class innovation and disruptive next-generation solutions.”

Plain

Say what the thing is, what changed and why it matters.

Grounded

Separate what is planned, implemented and validated.

Useful

Prefer concrete information over ceremonial corporate language.

08 · Digital & UI

The Group frames. Products express.

Group-level digital surfaces should remain editorial and minimal. Product applications may have stronger functional systems, but should inherit the Group's commitment to clarity, hierarchy and restraint.

Group surfaces

Editorial navigation

Use strong headings, clear sections, light interaction and responsive layouts. The Group website and reports are references for this direction.

Product surfaces

Function first

Each venture owns its UI language. Group consistency should appear in the quality of structure, not by forcing every product into the same colours or component library.

09 · Documents & presentations

One information design language.

Reports, presentations, proposals, one-pagers and internal documents should feel like the same organisation produced them, even when their formats differ.

Headlines

Condensed and decisive

Use IBM Plex Sans Condensed for major titles and section statements.

Structure

Evidence-led

Use clear metadata, thin rules, short summaries and visible distinction between status, evidence and commentary.

Colour

Signal sparingly

Gold can mark key messages, dates or structural accents. Do not turn every heading or table into a gold treatment.

10 · Portfolio ownership

“Powered by The 24th Group”

This line belongs on venture-level surfaces where Group ownership should be visible without making the parent brand the primary identity.

Reference treatment

Product first. Group second.

The venture logo remains the dominant signature. The ownership line should be small, quiet and consistently spaced. It is not used on Group-owned documents or Group-only surfaces.

Powered byThe 24th Group

Use it

On product websites, product documents, selected product footers, launch material and places where ownership context is useful.

Do not use it

On The 24th Group website, Group reports, Group presentations or anywhere the Group is already the primary brand.

11 · Co-branding

Keep the hierarchy obvious.

When multiple ventures or external partners appear together, avoid merging identities into a single invented lockup. Keep signatures separate and let layout establish the relationship.

Do

  • Keep each logo at a visually balanced size.
  • Use spacing or rules to show separation.
  • Lead with the brand responsible for the communication.
  • Use the Group ownership line quietly when appropriate.

Don't

  • Create hybrid marks.
  • Force venture logos into Group gold.
  • Place logos too close together.
  • Use the Group as a decorative sponsor badge on its own products.
12 · Guardrails

What it should never become

These guardrails protect the Group from drifting into generic corporate or startup visual language.

Keep

  • Minimal editorial layouts.
  • Strong black-and-white contrast.
  • Warm paper as a secondary surface.
  • Gold as a restrained signal.
  • Real evidence, real products and real operating context.
  • Clear typography and deliberate spacing.

Avoid

  • Gradients and glossy startup styling.
  • Generic venture-capital visual tropes.
  • Excessive rounded cards and shadows.
  • Stock corporate photography.
  • Hype-heavy language.
  • Making every venture visually identical to the Group.
