Oroko is a clarity tool. The brand should feel structured, calm and intelligently minimal, with the process itself always more important than the interface around it.

01 · Brand idea

Clarity before decoration

Structured

Hierarchy, alignment and relationships should be immediately legible.

Quiet

Oroko should not compete with the user’s process. Avoid loud colour, excessive cards and ornamental UI.

Operational

Design for real work, ownership, procedures, inputs, outputs and governance, not abstract whiteboarding.

02 · Identity

The mark should feel precise

Use

Keep the mark crisp, isolated and aligned. Do not add gradients, shadows or decorative process lines to the logo itself.

03 · Colour

Neutral surfaces, strong hierarchy

04 · Layout

Canvas first. Interface second.

Auto-layout

Prefer orderly, understandable process structures over freeform placement.

Inspector patterns

Details belong in clean side panels and structured records, not floating popovers everywhere.

Density with restraint

Information can be detailed without feeling crowded when spacing and hierarchy are consistent.

06 · Voice

Plain, useful and exact

Use process language

Steps, owners, decisions, procedures, inputs and outputs should be named plainly.

Avoid automation hype

Oroko documents and clarifies work. Do not position it as an everything-workflow engine.

Make complexity understandable

Explain without oversimplifying. Good Oroko language removes ambiguity.
